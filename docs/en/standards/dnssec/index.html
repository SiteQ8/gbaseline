Issue 2, 19 October 2026

Can a resolver prove that the domain's DNS answers are genuine?

Why it matters

Without DNSSEC, a forged DNS answer can send visitors and mail to an attacker's servers. With it, validating resolvers reject any answer that lacks a valid signature chain back to the root. A domain can only be signed end to end if the suffix above it is signed too.

When a domain passes

Passes when validating resolvers mark the domain's answers as authenticated.

How to fix it

  1. Turn on DNSSEC signing at your DNS host.
  2. Give the DS record to the body that runs the suffix above the domain, then confirm that validation succeeds.
  3. Where the suffix itself is not signed, ask the body that runs it to sign it, since no domain under it can validate until then.

An example record

example.com  DS  370 13 2 BE74359954660069D5C63D200C39F5603827D7DD02B56F120EE9F3A86764247C
