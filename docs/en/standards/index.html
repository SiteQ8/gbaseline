Thirteen standards

Thirteen standards in four groups, from the foundations to the frontier, each with a page on why it matters, when a domain passes and how to fix it.

Email

  • 18.9% Email spoofing protection (DMARC) Can anyone send email that claims to come from this domain?
  • 69.8% Authorised senders (SPF) Does the domain list the servers allowed to send its mail?
  • 0.5% Encrypted inbound mail (MTA-STS) Must other servers encrypt the mail they deliver to this domain?
  • 1.5% Mail encryption reports (TLS-RPT) Will the domain hear about failed encrypted deliveries?

DNS

  • 3.2% Signed DNS (DNSSEC) Can a resolver prove that the domain's DNS answers are genuine?
  • 2.2% Allowed certificate issuers (CAA) Does the domain name the certificate authorities allowed to issue for it?

Websites

  • 92.0% Valid HTTPS Does the website open over HTTPS with a valid certificate?
  • 80.6% Plain HTTP sends visitors to HTTPS Does typing the plain address end up on HTTPS?
  • 18.6% HTTPS enforced by the browser (HSTS) Will browsers refuse to load the site without HTTPS?
  • 86.5% Current TLS (1.3) Does the site speak TLS 1.3?
  • 67.8% Old TLS switched off (1.0 and 1.1) Does the site still accept TLS 1.0 or 1.1?
  • 52.2% Post quantum key exchange Is traffic sent today protected against decryption by a future quantum computer?

Vulnerability disclosure

  • 2.1% A way to report vulnerabilities (security.txt) Can a researcher find out how to report a vulnerability?
